## 3. Who the video is talking to

Practising SEOs, content strategists, and site owners with real experience. Three to
ten years in. They run projects, they read documentation, they already know what an
entity is in theory.

They are **not** beginners. Do not explain what SEO is. Do not define basic terms.
Speaking down to this audience kills the ad in the first two seconds.

What they feel, in their own words:

1. **Great content that still will not rank.** They publish thorough, well-researched
   pieces and watch them stall on page three, while thinner competitor pages sit at
   the top.
2. **Authority that dilutes instead of building.** They add more content to build
   depth, but every new post seems to weaken the site rather than strengthen it.
3. **Keywords one at a time, not topics.** They chase individual queries while
   competitors cover the whole topic as a system, and Google rewards them for it.
4. **They cannot explain what ranked.** When a page finally lands, they cannot tell
   if it was content, links, structure, or luck. So they cannot repeat it on purpose.

Point 4 is the sharpest one. A professional who cannot explain their own win is the
emotional core of this ad.

## 7. What is actually taught (six live modules)

1. **Foundations.** The core fundamentals of Semantic SEO: information retrieval,
   natural language processing, and how Google evaluates topical relevance beyond
   keyword density.
2. **Entities and SERP.** Identifying primary entities, mapping contextually relevant
   attributes, and structuring content so search engines parse your expertise
   unambiguously.
3. **Topical map architecture.** Core versus supporting clusters, clear boundaries,
   publishing sequence, and complete topical coverage that commands authority.
4. **Semantic content briefs.** Briefs built on entity relationships, contextual
   vectors, and intent fulfilment rather than word counts.
5. **Semantic internal linking.** Contextual link meshes that pass PageRank
   efficiently, signal topical relationships, and let pages rank together as a network.
6. **Visual semantics (preview).** Visual entity recognition, multimodal search
   signals, and how visual elements reinforce semantic context on modern SERPs.

If the video only has room for a few, use modules 1, 3, and 5. Entities, topical
maps, internal linking. That trio tells the whole story.

Framing line for this section:

> **Six live modules. An engineering framework you can execute Monday morning.**

## 11. Voice and tone

Confident, plain, and technical without jargon padding. The voice of a senior
practitioner talking to a peer.

- Short sentences. Concrete nouns. Active voice.
- Say the thing. Name the mechanism.
- **No em-dashes or en-dashes anywhere in the on-screen copy or narration.**
  Use a full stop, a colon, or a comma instead. Number ranges use "to".
- Never use: seamless, robust, powerful, cutting-edge, game-changing, leverage,
  unlock, supercharge, revolutionise, effortlessly, dive in, in today's fast-paced
  world, at its core.
- Never use the "this is not just X, it is Y" construction.
- No exclamation marks. No hype. No countdown-timer urgency theatre. The date is the
  urgency.

## 12. Visual direction

The brand is editorial and restrained. Think a well-set magazine feature, not a
software advertisement. The page it advertises is deep navy and warm cream with a
single terracotta accent, and the video has to look like it came from the same studio.

**Palette (use these exact values):**

| Role | Hex | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Deep navy, primary ground | `#0C1E2E` | Dark frames, the majority of the video |
| Off-white cream | `#FBFAF8` | Light frames, breathing room |
| Terracotta accent | `#E9AF88` | One accent per frame. Headline emphasis, the CTA |
| Muted slate | `#565D64` | Secondary and caption text |
| Hairline | `#E8E7E3` | Rules and dividers on light frames |
| Champagne (secondary) | `#C9B57A` | Sparingly, module labels only |
| Muted violet (secondary) | `#7A6E9C` | Sparingly, module labels only |

**Typography:**

- Headlines: **Figtree**, medium weight, tight tracking (roughly minus 2.6 percent),
  line height near 1.05. Large and calm.
- The emphasis phrase inside each headline is set in **Cormorant italic**, in
  terracotta. This is the single most recognisable brand device. Use it once per
  frame, never twice.
- Body and captions: Figtree regular, generous line height near 1.6.
- Small labels: uppercase, 10 to 11px equivalent, letter spacing near 0.15em.
- Numbers are tabular and set in Cormorant italic when used as ordinals (01, 02, 03).

**Composition rules:**

- Left-aligned type on a wide margin. Centre only the final CTA frame.
- One idea per frame. If a frame has two sentences competing, split it.
- Generous negative space. Do not fill the frame.
- Rounded corners on image and card blocks, roughly 16 to 24px equivalent.
- Faint dot-grid texture is allowed on navy frames at very low opacity, about 6
  percent, as a radial dot every 22px. Nothing louder than that.
- Photographs of Pavel sit against black or navy with the edges fading to the
  background rather than sitting in a hard box.

**Motion:**

- Slow. Long holds. Cuts on the beat of the sentence, not faster.
- Type enters with a small rise and a fade, roughly 300ms, ease out. No bounce, no
  spin, no zoom punches, no kinetic-typography chaos.
- Search Console charts should draw in left to right so the flat-then-compounding
  shape is felt rather than just shown.

**Never:**

- Stock imagery of laptops, handshakes, arrows going up, or a magnifying glass on a
  browser window.
- Generic tech-blue gradients, neon, glassmorphism, or 3D shapes.
- Emoji anywhere.
- More than one accent colour in a single frame.
- Fabricated dashboards, invented numbers, or fake Google interfaces.
